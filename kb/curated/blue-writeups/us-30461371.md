WoW: Forever has a new measure against real-money trading (buying or selling gold for cash): the Temporary Economy Hold. Kaivax explained it on October 13, after some players had already been hit by it.

An account suspected of being on either side of such a trade can be put on hold. While the hold lasts, it has restricted use of trade, mail, guild banks and the Auction House. Blizzard's aim is to stop suspect gold before it moves on or reaches the wider economy, and to buy time for a proper investigation.

There are two possible outcomes. If the activity turns out to be innocent, the hold lifts on its own. If it was real-money trading, the account faces action. Blizzard says ordinary players are unlikely ever to see a hold. The system is new, though, so some holds may be mistakes while it is tuned. A hold on its own leaves no permanent mark on the account.

In practice, if you suddenly can't trade, mail or use the Auction House, this may be why. The post doesn't say how long an investigation takes, how you are told about a hold, or whether you can appeal one. Those questions are still open.

Blizzard also says these systems will come to every version of World of Warcraft if they prove successful.
